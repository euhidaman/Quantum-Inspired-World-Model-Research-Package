4. Primary Mission
Develop a publication-grade research program that tests whether structured quantum-inspired latent beliefs can improve robot navigation and path planning in environments containing uncertain dynamic obstacles.
The central practical problem is:
> A robot must plan safely when moving obstacles have continuous, multimodal, correlated, and partially observed future trajectories, especially under occlusion, sensor noise, hidden intent, and interactions among multiple agents.
The central scientific question is:
> At matched compute and model capacity, do explicit structured latent beliefs over obstacle futures provide safer, better-calibrated, more sample-efficient, more interpretable, or more computationally useful planning than standard latent vectors, factorized stochastic states, object slots, particle sets, Gaussian mixtures, normalizing flows, diffusion predictors, and classical robotics planners?
The three principal approaches are:
Quantum Latent Core (QLC);
Purified Slot Core (PSC);
Trajectory Ensemble Core (TEC).
The Intensity Flow Core (IFC), random-finite-set methods, classical particle filters, Gaussian mixtures, normalizing flows, diffusion models, and conventional world models are important baselines or possible future extensions, but they are not the three main proposed approaches.
Do not combine all approaches into one large hybrid before testing them independently. The goal is to discover which approach, if any, deserves to become the main contribution.

7. Robotics Problem Formulation
Model navigation as a partially observable controlled dynamical system.
Let:
(x_t^R): ego robot state;
(m): static map or scene structure;
(N_t): number of dynamic obstacles at time (t);
(x_{j,t}): physical state of obstacle (j), such as position, heading, velocity, and acceleration;
(i_{j,t}): hidden intention, goal, policy, or behavior mode of obstacle (j);
(o_t): observation;
(a_t): robot action.
A scene state may be written as:
[
s_t = \left(x_t^R, m, {x_{j,t}, i_{j,t}}_{j=1}^{N_t}\right).
]
Meaning: the full state contains the robot, the static environment, and all moving obstacles together with hidden behavior variables.
The observation process is:
[
o_t \sim p(o_t \mid s_t).
]
Meaning: the robot sees only a noisy and partial view of the true state.
The Bayesian belief is:
[
b_t(s_t) = p(s_t \mid o_{0:t}, a_{0:t-1}).
]
Meaning: the belief is the probability distribution over possible current worlds given all past observations and actions.
The learned world model approximates this belief using a latent representation such as a vector, Gaussian, categorical state, density matrix, tensor network, or trajectory ensemble.
For planning horizon (H), the robot selects actions by minimizing expected task cost and risk:
[
a^*_{t:t+H-1}
\arg\min_a
\mathbb{E}\left[
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1} c(s_{t+h},a_{t+h})
\right]
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{risk}}\operatorname{Risk}(C_{\mathrm{collision}}).
]
Meaning: choose actions that reach the goal efficiently while penalizing dangerous predicted futures.
Investigate risk functions including:
expected collision cost;
chance constraints;
Conditional Value at Risk, CVaR;
worst-case cost over credible hypotheses;
distributionally robust risk;
information-aware or active-perception objectives.
A representative chance constraint is:
[
P(\text{collision within horizon }H) \le \delta.
]
Meaning: the planned route is acceptable only when predicted collision probability remains below threshold (\delta).
Do not select a risk measure because it sounds advanced. Compare its assumptions and usefulness for each latent representation.

8. Approach 1: Quantum Latent Core
8.1 State
Represent the belief as a density operator:
[
\rho_t \in \mathbb{C}^{d\times d},
\qquad
\rho_t=\rho_t^\dagger,
\qquad
\rho_t\succeq 0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}\rho_t=1.
]
Where:
(d): latent Hilbert-space dimension;
(\rho_t^\dagger): conjugate transpose;
(\rho_t\succeq 0): positive semidefinite;
(\operatorname{Tr}\rho_t=1): normalized total probability.
For numerical stability, optionally store a low-rank factor:
[
\rho_t = \frac{A_tA_t^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}(A_tA_t^\dagger)},
\qquad
A_t\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times r}.
]
Meaning: (A_tA_t^\dagger) is automatically Hermitian and positive semidefinite, and normalization gives unit trace.
Possible basis interpretations to research include:
obstacle-intention primitives;
trajectory prototypes;
joint room configurations;
interaction outcomes;
occupancy-flow modes.
Do not assume one interpretation is correct.
8.2 Readout
For a positive measurement operator (M_y):
[
p(y\mid\rho_t)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_tM_y),
]
with:
[
M_y\succeq0,
\qquad
\sum_yM_y=I.
]
Interpretation:
diagonal entries of (\rho) provide probabilities in one chosen basis;
off-diagonal entries encode coherence or relations between basis alternatives;
off-diagonal terms matter only if later dynamics or readouts make them operational.
8.3 Dynamics
A discrete completely positive trace-preserving update is:
[
\rho_{t+1}
\sum_kK_k(a_t,h_t)\rho_tK_k^\dagger(a_t,h_t),
]
subject to:
[
\sum_kK_k^\dagger K_k=I.
]
Where:
(K_k): learned Kraus operators;
(a_t): robot action;
(h_t): optional deterministic context.
A continuous-time GKSL equation may be considered only under its assumptions:
[
\frac{d\rho}{dt}
-i[H,\rho]
+
\sum_k\gamma_k
\left(
L_k\rho L_k^\dagger
\frac12{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho}
\right).
]
Interpretation:
(H): Hamiltonian-like reversible transition term;
(L_k): dissipative or uncertainty channels;
(\gamma_k): rates;
([H,\rho]): commutator;
({A,B}): anticommutator.
Required caveat:
GKSL characterizes linear Markovian quantum dynamical semigroups;
state-dependent nonlinear neural dynamics are not covered by the basic uniqueness theorem;
do not invoke GKSL outside its assumptions.
8.4 Capacity claim
A categorical distribution over (d) outcomes has (d-1) independent real parameters.
A general density matrix has (d^2-1) real degrees of freedom.
This is only a parameterization statement. It is not proof of greater observable process expressiveness or better prediction.
The correct QLC question is:
> Do operational off-diagonal terms improve prediction, calibration, filtering, or planning compared with diagonal \(\rho\) and equally sized classical models?
8.5 Prior-art and risk analysis
Research and verify:
hidden quantum Markov models;
observable operator models;
predictive-state representations;
Born machines;
density-matrix neural networks;
quantum-inspired sequence models;
finite-dimensional HQMM expressiveness;
identifiability of coherence from classical sensors.
Do not claim QLC is the first density-matrix sequence model.
Main failure modes:
the learned state becomes effectively diagonal;
off-diagonal terms do not affect decoded predictions;
a classical OOM, Gaussian mixture, flow, particle model, or diffusion model matches it;
coherence is basis-dependent and not identifiable;
fixed trace makes variable cardinality awkward;
linear-channel restrictions limit modeling flexibility.
8.6 Decisive QLC experiment
Construct latent states with:

different off-diagonal entries.
Apply:
a diagonal or commuting transition/readout;
a basis-changing transition/readout.
Test whether:

If no operational difference appears, QLC should remain a theoretical baseline or be abandoned as the principal contribution.

9. Approach 2: Purified Slot Core
9.1 State
Use (L) potential object slots.
Each slot may contain:
existence;
position or pose code;
velocity code;
intention code;
semantic class;
visibility;
trajectory primitive;
interaction state.
Define:
(L): maximum number of trackable slots;
(d): local slot dimension;
(\chi): bond dimension controlling cross-slot correlation;
(\kappa): purification or Kraus dimension controlling mixedness.
Represent the joint belief using a locally purified tensor-network state. A schematic representation is:
[
\rho = \frac{XX^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}(XX^\dagger)},
]
where (X) is a tensor network with physical, purification, and bond indices.
A rough chain-like storage scaling is:
[
O(Ld\kappa\chi^2).
]
The full joint basis contains:
[
d^L
]
possible configurations.
This does not mean that every distribution over all (d^L) configurations is freely represented at small (\chi).
The hypothesis is:
> A modest bond dimension may compactly encode the local and group correlations that dominate physical scenes.
This is an area-law-like empirical hypothesis, not a proven property of robotics scenes.
9.2 Capacity dial
Conceptually:
(\chi=1): slots are independent;
larger (\chi): stronger cross-slot dependencies can be represented;
very large (\chi): approach the dense joint state at prohibitive cost.
Verify the exact entropy or mutual-information bound for the selected tensor-network topology. Do not copy a bound from pure MPS to LPDO, MPO, PEPS, tree, or graph states without checking applicability.
9.3 Robotics interpretation
Bonds may represent dependencies such as:
two pedestrians moving together;
one agent yielding to another;
mutually exclusive doorway passage;
a cart and operator moving together;
one obstacle occluding another;
multiple agents responding to the same signal;
leader-follower behavior;
joint collision avoidance.
The key hypothesis is that independent slots may assign probability to impossible combinations, while bonded slots may suppress them.
9.4 Dynamics
A local-channel update with truncation is:
[
\rho_{t+1}
\mathcal{T}_\chi
\left[
\sum_kK_k(a_t,h_t)\rho_tK_k^\dagger(a_t,h_t)
\right].
]
Interpretation:
local or pairwise gates update slots;
dynamics generate correlation;
bond dimensions may grow;
the state is truncated back to (\chi);
discarded weight measures lost structure.
Do not hide that the channel is linear in (\rho). Truncation is state-dependent but partly an approximation mechanism.
9.5 Inference and sensor fusion
Attach sensor likelihood factors to relevant slots and use belief propagation or tensor contraction to estimate marginals.
Investigate:
exact inference on tree graphs;
loopy belief propagation;
damping;
generalized belief propagation;
neural message-passing approximations;
contraction complexity;
convergence diagnostics.
Do not claim BP is exact on general loopy graphs.
9.6 Representation-native capacity alarm
Track discarded weight during truncation.
Candidate hypothesis:
> A spike in discarded weight predicts that the scene contains more dependency structure than the selected \(\chi\) can retain, making discarded weight a representation-level capacity alarm.
This must be calibrated against actual rollout and planning error.
9.7 Decisive PSC experiment
Construct a minimal two-agent task whose futures are correlated.
Examples:
both yield or both continue;
two group members move together;
exactly one moving is impossible;
two doorway choices are mutually exclusive.
Compare:
factorized categorical latent;
PSC with (\chi=1);
PSC with (\chi=2);
PSC with (\chi=4);
dense-joint oracle;
classical mixture or particle baseline.
Primary diagnostic:
[
P(\text{impossible joint configurations}).
]
PSC survives only if increasing (\chi) reduces impossible joint mass and improves planning at acceptable compute cost.

10. Approach 3: Trajectory Ensemble Core
10.1 State
Represent the latent as a weighted measure over pure-state branches:
[
\mu_t
\sum_{m=1}^{M}w_{m,t},\delta_{[\psi_{m,t}]},
]
where:
[
\psi_m\in\mathbb{C}^d,
\qquad
|\psi_m|=1,
\qquad
\psi_m\sim e^{i\theta}\psi_m,
\qquad
\sum_mw_m=1.
]
The pure-state geometry is complex projective space:
[
\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}.
]
The corresponding density matrix is:
[
\rho_t
\sum_mw_{m,t}|\psi_{m,t}\rangle\langle\psi_{m,t}|.
]
Thus QLC is the first moment or average of TEC.
10.2 Robotics interpretation
Each branch should represent one complete plausible scene future, such as:
pedestrian crosses before the robot;
pedestrian yields;
person stops behind an occluder;
cart turns into the corridor;
group splits;
second obstacle emerges from a doorway.
A branch is a complete joint scene hypothesis, not one obstacle point.
Weights are classical probabilities across branches.
10.3 Central theoretical danger
Different ensembles can produce the same density matrix.
Under branch-independent linear CPTP evolution and Born-rule readout, ensembles with the same (\rho) are operationally indistinguishable.
TEC adds useful capacity only if one or more of the following is true:
dynamics depend on branch identity;
planning directly uses branch-specific predictions;
branch labels carry persistent environmental meaning;
control uses information not recoverable from (\rho).
Do not hide this issue.
10.4 Branch-conditioned dynamics
A general branch update may be written as:
[
\psi_{m,t+1}
\frac{F_\theta(\psi_{m,t},a_t,h_t,\mu_t)}
{|F_\theta(\psi_{m,t},a_t,h_t,\mu_t)|}.
]
Weights may update through observation likelihoods:
[
w'm
\propto
w_m,p(o{t+1}\mid\psi_{m,t+1}).
]
This is not automatically a standard quantum channel. Branch-conditioned dynamics deliberately move beyond ordinary branch-independent GKSL evolution.
10.5 Planning with branches
For each candidate action sequence, roll out every branch:
[
\tau_m(a_{t:t+H-1}).
]
A risk-aware objective may use:
[
J(a)
\sum_mw_mC(\tau_m(a))
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{risk}}
\operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha({C(\tau_m(a))}).
]
An active-perception objective may reward actions that separate competing hypotheses:
[
I(a)
\sum_{m<n}w_mw_n,d^2(\psi_m(a),\psi_n(a)).
]
Compare candidate distances:
Fubini-Study distance;
decoded trajectory distance;
expected observation divergence;
mutual information;
Jensen-Shannon divergence.
Do not assume Fubini-Study distance aligns with robot task value.
10.6 Decisive TEC experiment
Construct two different branch ensembles with the same density matrix.
Compare:
branch-independent dynamics;
branch-conditioned dynamics;
density-matrix average;
matched classical particles.
Measure:
whether decoded rollouts diverge;
whether divergence is correct;
whether branch-aware planning selects safer or more informative actions;
whether ordinary particles achieve the same effect with less complexity.
If same-(\rho) ensembles remain behaviorally identical, TEC is redundant and should not be the main contribution.

13. Research Questions and Hypotheses
Treat all hypotheses as provisional.
RQ1: Structured uncertainty
Does explicit structured uncertainty improve dynamic-obstacle prediction compared with implicit or sampled uncertainty?
H1: At matched compute, at least one structured core improves calibrated prediction under occlusion.
Null: Gaussian, mixture, particle, diffusion, categorical, or feature-based baselines perform equally well.
RQ2: Cross-obstacle correlation
Does explicit cross-obstacle dependence improve planning when behaviors interact?
H2: PSC with sufficient (\chi) reduces impossible joint futures and collision rate compared with independent slots.
Null: attention, particles, mixtures, or standard graph models learn the same dependencies.
RQ3: Operational QLC coherence
Do QLC off-diagonal terms have a measurable effect?
H3: full QLC outperforms diagonal (\rho) when basis-changing dynamics are required;
Null: the model becomes diagonal or a classical model matches it.
RQ4: Persistent branch identity
Does preserving branch identity improve planning?
H4: TEC provides safer or more informative action choices than its density-matrix average and matched classical particles;
Null: branch identity is gauge or classical ensembles provide the same benefit.
RQ5: Native diagnostics
Can representation-native diagnostics predict planning failure?
H5: QLC entropy or purity, PSC discarded weight, and TEC effective branch count or spread predict rollout and collision failure;
Null: they add no value beyond residual error or ordinary ensemble variance.
RQ6: Scaling
Do benefits survive increases in:
forecast horizon;
obstacle count;
occlusion duration;
behavioral correlation;
environmental shift;
sensor noise?
H6: the best structured latent retains an advantage;
Null: compute and approximation error erase the advantage.
